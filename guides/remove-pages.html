Remove Unwanted Pages From a PDF Document

Keep the Rest

Some documents arrive with clutter attached: a blank sheet the scanner produced, a fax cover page, an internal notes section that should not go to a client. The document is otherwise fine and just needs a few pages gone. This tool takes one PDF and a list of the pages to exclude, and produces removed-pages.pdf containing everything else. Numbered page previews help you confirm you are pointing at the right sheets. The single biggest risk here is not technical: it is numbering.

Open Remove Pages

Common cleanups

  • Clearing blank pages from a scan: duplex scanning a stack of single-sided sheets produces an empty page between every real one.
  • Stripping an internal section: a proposal with pricing notes or reviewer comments that should not leave the building.
  • Removing a cover or fax sheet: documents from an agency often carry a routing page nobody needs to keep.
  • Cleaning up after a merge: a combined file that ended up with a duplicated page or an accidental extra document at the end.
  • Cutting an outdated appendix: a superseded schedule or price list sitting at the back of an otherwise current document.

Two libraries, two jobs

PDF.js renders the previews. It draws each page as a numbered thumbnail so you can look at what you are about to remove. Those previews exist purely for checking your selection; they are not what gets written into your new file.

pdf-lib builds the result. It parses your document, creates a new empty PDF, and copies across every page you did not list for removal. Those pages are copied as PDF pages rather than redrawn, so they are not intentionally rasterised or recompressed: selectable text stays selectable and vector artwork stays sharp.

In other words, you look at pictures of pages, but the file you download is built from the pages themselves. Selection is made by typing the page numbers to remove into the "Pages to remove" field; the numbered thumbnails are your reference for getting those numbers right.

Why page numbering causes most of the trouble

A PDF has two sets of page numbers, and they usually disagree.

File position is what this tool uses: the first page in the document is page 1, the next is page 2, and so on, regardless of what appears on them.

The printed number is part of the page artwork. Reports typically begin numbering after a cover, a contents page, and perhaps a summary, so the page showing "1" might be the fourth page of the file. Enter numbers from the printed sequence and you will remove pages three or four positions away from the ones you meant. The numbered previews are the reliable reference: count the pages you can see, from the start.

The selection rules

Accepted

  • Comma-separated numbers: 3,8,11
  • Ascending ranges, inclusive at both ends: 7-9 removes 7, 8, and 9
  • A combination: 1,5-8,12

Rejected

  • Blank input.
  • Descending ranges such as 9-4.
  • Non-integer values.
  • Numbers beyond the end of the document.

Duplicates are ignored. Listing the same page twice has the same effect as listing it once, since a page can only be removed once.

You cannot remove every page. A selection covering the whole document is rejected, because an empty PDF is not a useful outcome and the tool will not produce one.

2

Enter the numbers

Type them into "Pages to remove", separated by commas. Ranges use a hyphen and run low to high: 2,7-9,14 removes 2, 7, 8, 9, and 14.

3

Generate and confirm

Download removed-pages.pdf, open it, and check that what disappeared is what you intended and nothing else went with it.

What the output looks like

You receive one file named removed-pages.pdf, containing all the pages you did not list, in their original relative order. Nothing is reordered. The retained pages are copied rather than re-rendered, so they look exactly as they did and there is no deliberate quality change.

No compression is applied, which means the result is not dramatically smaller unless the pages you removed were the heavy ones. Your source PDF is untouched, so if the result is not what you wanted, start again from the original.

Removing pages or extracting them?

These are mirror-image operations, and choosing correctly saves a lot of typing.

Remove Pages, this tool, keeps everything except what you name. Use it when the majority of the document is staying: dropping four pages from a sixty-page report means typing four numbers.

Extract Pages keeps only what you name and builds a new document from those pages in the order you typed. Use it when the majority is going. The test is simply which list is shorter, with one functional difference: Extract Pages lets you set the output order, while removal preserves the original sequence of whatever remains.

Limitations worth knowing

  • Document-level features may not transfer: bookmarks, outlines, attachments, metadata, form fields, and cross-page links live at document level and may not survive into the new file. Inspect the output if your document depends on any of those.
  • Selection only: pages cannot be reordered, rotated, cropped, or altered here, only kept or dropped.
  • No content removal within a page: sensitive text in the middle of a page cannot be taken out by removing pages; the whole sheet goes or stays.
  • One document at a time: there is no batch processing across several files.
  • Unreadable files fail: encrypted, restricted, or damaged PDFs may not open, since the libraries need to read the file to preview and copy from it.
  • Long documents are demanding: rendering a thumbnail for every page and building the new document both happen in the browser, so a very long file may be slow or may fail on a modest device.

Privacy and data handling

File contents are processed in browser memory and are not sent to WeConvertFiles for conversion. PDF.js and pdf-lib are downloaded from third-party CDNs when needed, so the workflow is not described as fully offline.

Troubleshooting

The wrong pages were removed

Printed page numbers were almost certainly used instead of file positions. Go back to the original, count from the previews, and try again.

My selection was rejected

Look for a descending range, a decimal, a number larger than the page count, or an empty field. Also check you have not accidentally listed every page.

A page I wanted gone is still there

Either it was outside your listed range, or a range boundary was off by one. Ranges are inclusive at both ends: 7-9 removes 7, 8, and 9.

The new file is barely smaller

Nothing is compressed. Removing light text pages from a scan-heavy document changes very little. If size is the goal, the Compress PDF tool addresses that directly, with its quality trade-off.

Previews take a long time to appear

Every page has to be rendered to show a thumbnail. On a long document this is heavy work; give it time, or close other tabs.

Frequently asked questions

Does removing pages change the quality of the ones that remain?

No. Retained pages are copied as PDF pages, not re-rendered as images, so they are not deliberately rasterised or recompressed. They look exactly as they did in the source.

Can I remove all the blank pages automatically?

There is no automatic blank-page detection. You identify them from the numbered previews and list their numbers. For an alternating scan, the blanks usually fall on a predictable pattern, which makes the list quick to build.

Will the remaining pages be renumbered?

Their positions in the file shift, so the second page of the new document is whatever followed the removed first page. Numbers printed on the pages themselves are artwork and stay as they were, which can leave visible gaps in the printed sequence.

Can I get the removed pages back as a separate file?

Not from this tool, which only produces the reduced document. If you want the excluded pages kept separately, run Extract Pages on the original with the same list of numbers before removing them.

Why can I not remove every page?

A PDF with no pages serves no purpose, so a selection covering the whole document is rejected. If you want rid of the whole document, delete the file instead.

Is the original file altered on my device?

No. It is read but never written to, so your source document is safe regardless of what the output looks like.
