Add Page Numbers to a PDF in the Correct Order

Footer Numbering

Page numbers make long documents easier to navigate, review, print, and discuss. The Add Page Numbers tool places a centered footer on every page: choose simple numbers such as 1, 2, 3, or the descriptive Page 1 of 5 format. The result downloads separately as page-numbers.pdf, leaving the source unchanged. The style is fixed: Helvetica at 10 points, dark gray, centered, 24 PDF points above the bottom edge, starting at 1.

Open Add Page Numbers

Quick answer. Finalize the PDF's page order, choose either simple numbering or Page X of Y, and generate the numbered copy. Check existing footer content, because position, font, size, color, starting number, and page selection are not customizable.

Why add page numbers to a PDF?

Make reviews more precise

Instead of "the paragraph near the end," a reviewer can identify "page 18." Stable references make feedback easier to understand and resolve.

Keep printed pages in order

Pages can be separated during printing, scanning, meetings, or distribution. Numbering provides an immediate sequence.

Prepare reports and submissions

Applications, proposals, exhibits, and administrative packets often benefit from continuous numbering across the final combined document.

Show progress through a document

The Page X of Y style tells readers both their current location and the total length of the PDF.

Improve meeting and classroom use

Participants can quickly turn to the same page during a presentation, lesson, workshop, or discussion.

Available numbering styles

StyleExample on page 3 of a 12-page PDFBest suited to
Simple3Minimal reports, handouts, and documents where the total is not needed
Page X of YPage 3 of 12Submissions, review copies, and packets where total length matters

Both styles begin with page 1 and number every physical page in the loaded PDF. The tool does not currently offer Roman numerals, alphabetic numbering, custom prefixes, section numbering, Bates numbering, or an offset starting value.

Exact page-number appearance

  • Font: Helvetica
  • Font size: 10 PDF points
  • Color: dark gray, approximately RGB 0.25, 0.28, 0.33
  • Horizontal position: centered according to the measured label width
  • Vertical position: 24 PDF points above the bottom edge
  • Pages included: every page
  • Starting value: 1
  • Output filename: page-numbers.pdf

These settings create a restrained footer suitable for documents with a clear bottom margin. They can overlap content in PDFs whose original design already uses the bottom center of the page.

Do PDF points equal screen pixels?

No. PDF coordinates use points, traditionally based on 72 points per inch. The tool places the number at a PDF-coordinate value of 24 from the bottom edge — roughly one third of an inch, although actual physical output can depend on page scaling and printer settings.

The label is positioned relative to each page's own width, so pages with different widths still receive a horizontally centered number.

Number pages only after the sequence is final

Page numbering should usually be one of the last structural steps. If pages are moved, inserted, deleted, or duplicated afterward, the visible numbers can stop matching the physical sequence. A recommended order of operations:

  1. 1. Gather every required source document.
  2. 2. Merge separate PDFs.
  3. 3. Remove unwanted pages.
  4. 4. Organize the final page order.
  5. 5. Rotate sideways or upside-down pages.
  6. 6. Confirm the final page count.
  7. 7. Add page numbers.
  8. 8. Apply a final watermark if required.
  9. 9. Inspect and distribute the finished file.

If the source already contains printed page numbers, decide whether new continuous numbering will clarify the document or create confusion.

How to add page numbers to a PDF

  1. 1. Open and inspect the source. Confirm all required pages are present and in final order, orientation is correct, no unwanted blank pages remain, the bottom-center area has clear space, and existing footers will not conflict.
  2. 2. Choose the numbering style. Use 1, 2, 3 for a compact footer, or Page 1 of 5 when the total count helps readers confirm the document is complete. The chosen style applies consistently to every page.
  3. 3. Generate the numbered PDF. Start the operation. The tool loads the document, calculates each label, places it on every page, and creates page-numbers.pdf.
  4. 4. Review the download. Inspect the first and last pages, portrait and landscape pages, pages with existing footers, pages of different sizes, full-bleed or dark backgrounds, and pages likely to be printed. Confirm the visible numbers correspond to the physical page sequence.
  5. 5. Keep the original. The numbered result is a separate copy. Retain the unnumbered source until the final version is accepted.

How the tool places page numbers

For every page, the implementation:

  1. 1. Loads the PDF into browser memory.
  2. 2. Embeds the standard Helvetica font.
  3. 3. Counts the total number of pages.
  4. 4. Creates either the current page number or a Page X of Y label.
  5. 5. Measures that label at a font size of 10.
  6. 6. Subtracts the measured width from the page width and divides by two.
  7. 7. Draws the text at that horizontal position and at y = 24.
  8. 8. Uses a fixed dark gray color, then saves the result as page-numbers.pdf.

Measuring each label before positioning it keeps 1, 10, and Page 10 of 100 centered even though their widths differ.

Existing footer content and overlap

The tool does not detect whether something already occupies the bottom center. Potential conflicts include existing page numbers, confidentiality notices, document titles, revision identifiers, copyright text, website addresses, printer marks, signatures or stamps, and full-page photographs.

If overlap occurs, the current interface cannot move the added number to a corner or change its color, size, or vertical margin. Possible alternatives:

  1. 1. Add blank margin space in a full PDF editor.
  2. 2. Remove or cover an obsolete existing footer before numbering.
  3. 3. Use software that supports custom page-number placement.
  4. 4. Keep the source unnumbered when overlap would make the result unreadable.

Numbering PDFs with mixed page sizes

A PDF can contain A4, Letter, Legal, landscape, and custom-sized pages in one document. The tool centers each number using that page's individual width, so the horizontal position adapts automatically.

The vertical position remains 24 points from the bottom edge on every page. On a very short page or a page with an unusual crop box, the label may appear too close to content or outside the useful visible area. Always review each distinct page size in the output.

Cover pages, front matter, and custom starting numbers

Many formal documents use numbering rules such as no visible number on the cover, Roman numerals for front matter, Arabic numbering beginning at the first chapter, a starting number continuing from an earlier volume, or different prefixes for exhibits and appendices.

The current tool does not implement those publishing conventions. It begins at 1 and numbers every page using the selected style. If a document needs complex numbering, use desktop publishing software or a full PDF editor that supports page labels, ranges, sections, and offsets.

Visible numbers versus PDF page labels

Visible page number

Text is drawn on the page itself. It appears on screen and usually prints with the document. This is what the WeConvertFiles tool adds.

PDF page label

Metadata can tell a viewer to display page names such as iii, A-12, or Cover in its navigation without drawing them onto the page. The tool does not create a custom page-label scheme.

Does adding page numbers reduce quality?

The operation draws a small text label onto existing pages. It does not intentionally render each complete page to a JPEG merely to add the footer, so existing text, images, and vector graphics are not deliberately rasterized.

However, the PDF is modified and saved as a new document. Complex forms, signatures, scripts, annotations, accessibility structures, or unusual PDF features should still be checked.

Page numbers and digitally signed PDFs

Adding a page number changes the PDF after it was signed. A reader may report that the cryptographic signature is invalid or that the document has been modified since signing. A recommended approach:

  1. 1. Finalize pages and numbering before the signing step.
  2. 2. Keep an already signed original unchanged.
  3. 3. Create a numbered derivative only when authorized.
  4. 4. Do not represent the derivative as the untouched signed document.
  5. 5. Re-sign the finalized copy through the approved process when required.

A visible page number does not create or replace a digital signature.

Limitations to understand

  • Every page is numbered. There is no page-range field and no option to skip a cover or blank page.
  • Numbering always begins at 1. The interface does not accept a custom starting number.
  • Placement is fixed. Numbers appear at the bottom center. Top, left, right, inside, outside, and alternating positions are not available.
  • Style is fixed. Font, size, color, and margin cannot be changed.
  • Only two label formats are available. Simple numbers or Page X of Y. Roman numerals, prefixes, suffixes, and section labels are not supported.
  • Existing content is not automatically avoided. The tool does not inspect the footer area or move labels around existing text and graphics.

Privacy and data handling

The PDF contents are processed in browser memory and are not sent to WeConvertFiles for numbering. Required JavaScript libraries may be downloaded from third-party content-delivery networks when the tool opens, so the workflow should not be described as completely offline.

If a visitor consents to site analytics, separate usage information such as visits, clicks, device details, or conversion events may be collected. Those analytics do not receive the contents of the PDF.

For sensitive documents, also consider device security, browser extensions, downloaded files, backups, shared folders, and the final distribution method.

Troubleshooting

The page number overlaps an existing footer

The current placement is fixed. Use a source with a clear bottom-center margin or a different PDF editor that supports custom positioning.

The cover page should not have a number

The tool numbers every page. A more advanced editor is required to skip selected pages or begin numbering later.

The numbering should begin at another value

Custom starting numbers are not supported. The first physical page always receives 1.

Numbers are difficult to see on a dark page

The text color is fixed dark gray. The current interface cannot add a light background plate or choose white text.

The numbers do not match the table of contents

Finalize page order before numbering and update the table of contents when necessary. Visible physical page numbers and pre-existing internal labels can differ.

A landscape page looks different

The label is centered using that page's width and remains 24 points above its bottom edge. Review every distinct orientation and page size.

A digital signature became invalid

Adding numbers modifies the PDF. Return to the original signed document and use an approved finalize-then-sign workflow.

The browser cannot process the document

Verify that the source opens normally. Close other memory-heavy tabs and retry. Protected, damaged, very large, or unusually structured PDFs may fail.

Frequently asked questions

Can I add page numbers to every page of a PDF?

Yes. The tool numbers every page, beginning with 1.

Which page-number styles are available?

Choose simple numbers such as 1, 2, 3, or labels such as Page 1 of 5.

Can I skip the cover page?

No. The current tool applies a number to every page.

Can numbering start at a value other than 1?

No. A custom starting value or offset is not supported.

Where are page numbers placed?

They are centered horizontally and positioned 24 PDF points above the bottom edge.

Can I move the number to the top or corner?

No. Placement is fixed at the bottom center.

Can I change the font, size, or color?

No. The tool uses Helvetica at 10 points in a fixed dark gray color.

Does adding page numbers reduce PDF quality?

The operation adds a text label and does not intentionally rasterize complete pages. Important documents should still be inspected after saving.

Will existing digital signatures remain valid?

Adding numbers changes the PDF and can invalidate or alter the status of an existing cryptographic signature.

Does the tool overwrite my original PDF?

No. It creates a separate file named page-numbers.pdf.
